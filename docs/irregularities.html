IR-47-001 The nodata value in training_features.tif is −3.4028234663852886e+38 — the float32 minimum — not NaN. np.isfinite() returns True for it. Any statistics, rank transform or model fitted without special-casing it treats −3.4e38 as a real measurement. All 19 bands are affected; 3,061 pixels inside the footprint carry it. A first pass at the band statistics produced mean = −inf for every band and an overflow warning. Every layer load tests v > SENTINEL with SENTINEL = −1e30. Recorded in evidence/training_band_stats.json as missing_in_fp. MEDIUM
IR-47-002 The 0.2778 participant score is not authenticated to H33-2-B2. A sibling repository records attribution_conflict_site_says_unscored_official_board_has_unlinked_0.2778_row; its page labels h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros unscored. H33-2-B2 is a hash-pinned reference raster whose geometry can be compared descriptively with other masks; it is not a controlled deletion experiment. The public leaderboard does not identify TIFFs, and using 0.2778 as an assumed score changes exploratory LOO fits without validating or falsifying the flank hypothesis. Keep the association unresolved. The sensitivity sweep is conditional: the coarse grid is positive at assumed DTI 0.2200 and negative by 0.2400; 0.2200 is not an exact break-even. The leaderboard cannot resolve the mapping. Manual review requires an organizer receipt or explicit, reliable owner evidence tying the exact TIFF hash to the score. HIGH
IR-47-003 sample_submission.tif is not an all-zero template. It contains 60,988 pixels at value 1, and every one of them coincides with labels == 1. Anyone who uploads the sample unchanged gets a submission consisting purely of masked catalogue pixels — mass that earns nothing. It also means the template cannot be used as a “blank” baseline for differencing. Recorded and asserted in src/gems47/grid.py::load_template. Not treated as a baseline anywhere. LOW
IR-47-004 The organiser’s reference solution does not optimise the leaderboard metric. drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution trains a U-Net with smp.losses.TverskyLoss(alpha=0.2, beta=0.8, mode="binary") — a patch-level, unweighted, undistanced Tversky loss. The notebook contains no DTI scorer at all: no k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0), no 300 m buffer, no global max-over-neighbours reduction. Patch-level Tversky and global distance-weighted Tversky have different optima. A model trained on the reference loss is not optimised for the score it is judged by. This is a real opportunity and also a trap for anyone who assumes the reference solution is score-aligned. Documented in knowledge/02_the_metric_algebra.md. The DTI here is implemented independently from the published formulas and verified against a literal O(N²) transcription and the organiser’s worked example. MEDIUM

IR-47-009 Two sibling downloads were verified defective. A “smooth-max-coverage” raster had 57.8 % of its 44,090 dots off the belief support it claimed to pack, and a catalogue-proxy DTI of 0.0163 against its own associated reported reference score of 0.1618. Root cause: blurring a 1-px binary ridge by σ = 1.85 px and then running max-coverage on the blur shifts the peak. A file like that scores near zero. It was quarantined upstream but is still publicly downloadable. The rule is not reproduced. Emission here enforces support confinement (a dot may only be placed where the belief field nominates a fault) and recomputes the marginal gain against credit already delivered, never against a static upper bound. IR-47-010 An arithmetic error propagated through several sibling repositories. The marginal credit bar was written as α·s/(1 − α·s) = 0.0549 at s = 0.26. The correct value is α·s = 0.0520 — the published form is 5.5 % too high. Every emission threshold built on the wrong form stops 5.5 % too early, leaving earned credit on the table. Re-derived from first principles in metric.marginal_gain and asserted in tests/test_metric.py: breakeven_k(0.26) == 0.0520, and the general rule dT·(1/DTI − α) > α·dF reduces to k > α·DTI for dT = k, dF = 1 − k. MEDIUM

IR-47-014 Two bugs in this project’s own first pass, both caught by the review passes and both of which would have produced impossible numbers. (a) The first-order Φ ≈ ⟨q, a⟩ is an upper bound, not an estimate: it drove FPw to −47,502 and the predicted DTI to 1.6459. Measured over-estimate +0.8 % / +7.8 % / +87.4 % at q ~ 0.002 / 0.02 / 0.2. (b) The dense kernel shift filled out-of-grid positions with 0.0, which for 1 − q means certain hidden truth beyond every border; it inflated E[κ] along all four edges and made the dense and sparse paths disagree by 29 %. Both fixed. Φ now uses the exact Bernoulli ring-product expectation in the numerically stable form E[κ] = 1 − Σ(kₘ − kₘ₊₁)Qₘ, guaranteed in [0,1]; the fill is 1.0; dense and sparse agree to 1e-10; a hard assertion fails the build if FPw ever goes negative; and the exact expectation is validated against 600-draw Monte-Carlo at three densities (z = −0.58, +0.28, −0.17). FIXED
