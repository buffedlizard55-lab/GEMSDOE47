Hypothesis

H51 uses the product of the slope anomaly at two regional scales as the detection field:

  1. Broad anomaly: Band 19 (det_elev_slope) minus its 25 px (2.5 km) Gaussian regional level
  2. Narrow anomaly: Band 19 minus its 12 px (1.2 km) Gaussian regional level
  3. Product: Both anomalies must be positive — the slope must be steeper than both regional levels
  4. Rank scaling: The product is rank-transformed to [0,1] within the evaluated domain

Why this works: A fault scarp creates a slope anomaly that persists across scales — it stands out from both the 2.5 km and 1.2 km regional backgrounds. Erosional features like stream banks and canyon rims create sharp but small-scale anomalies that don't persist at the broader scale. The product suppresses these false positives while preserving genuine fault scarps.

Physical basis: This is the multi-scale persistence concept from Frangi et al. (1998) Multiscale vessel enhancement filtering, adapted from Hessian eigenvalue analysis to the slope anomaly field. In the Basin and Range province, normal fault scarps are the primary structural expression of geothermal systems (Faulds et al. 2021, NBMG Report 58).
