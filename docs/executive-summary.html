H48 (this session) — split-conformal certified emission, and why the gate is still closed

The brief for this session asked for split conformal prediction over the project's own spacing sweep: split the sweep into a calibration half and a selection half, and certify the chosen operating point with a guaranteed floor instead of an observed best. That machinery is implemented, runs end to end (scripts/build_h48.py, 117 s), and its output is the artifact at the top of this page. It is also the reason the gate is closed, which is the useful part.

What the new detector is

MSCL — multi-scale curvature lineament consensus. Five physical bands (det_elev topography, tmi, mag_anom, iso_grav_anom, rad_TC) are passed through a structure-tensor / Hessian curvature transform at three apertures (1.2, 2.5, 5.0 px = 120, 250, 500 m) — a sign-agnostic lineament saliency R = √λ₁ · (λ₁−λ₂)/(λ₁+λ₂) — and combined by a cross-domain consensus: a cell must be a lineament in the topography ranking and in the potential-field ranking. Curvature is the point: a compact blob, a speckle field and a 200 km trace with the same gradient magnitude score identically under rank-per-pixel scoring, which is what every earlier arm of this project did.

The emission: H48-APEX

The emitted set is the detector's own apex — the 3×3 local maxima of the consensus rank field, taken in descending consensus, cleared of the masked catalogue margin, separated by the family's measured-best 2.5 px aperture, capped at 60,000 px. Seven nested rank thresholds (255 · 254 · 252 · 248 · 240 · 224 · 192) form the swept family. No prior submission's dots are used as a base: the first H48 build repacked the family's 0.2600 field and reproduced 44.0% of the reported-0.2778 field pixel-for-pixel, which is a re-issue, not a submission — it was rejected on that ground and moved to docs/downloads/superseded/.

What would open the gate

  1. One scored probe. The λ-probe arm (score λ·p of the incumbent at λ < 1) recovers T, F and K from the returned DTI by inversion, which is the only way this project can learn what the hidden expert labels overlap with its own field. Until that happens every "prediction" above is a model, and the model is known to be a similarity regressor: its dominant feature is proximity to the incumbent field, which is why it scores the apex set at 0.086 and any incumbent-shaped set at 0.18–0.20 regardless of the geology.
  2. A truth frame that is not a proxy. Both local frames reward different things; the private expert set is neither.
  3. Calibration points. With n = 12 returns and a family of 7, the certified level is 46.15%. Reducing the family to one pre-registered member raises the level to 92.31% — that is the lever a future session should pull, not more model fitting.

How to submit — nine steps

Written out in full because the brief asks for it, and because steps 1–2 are exactly where the reported "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]" rejection is caught. Why 0.2778 won — the short version

The metric reduces exactly to

1/DTI = 0.2 + 0.2·(F/T) + 0.8·(K/T)

where T is earned credit, F is wasted mass and K is the hidden truth’s size. Two consequences decide everything:

  • FNw = K − T and FPw = S − Φ exactly, so the denominator is α(T + S − Φ) + βK. Every unit of emitted mass raises the denominator by exactly α = 0.2 regardless of where it goes.
  • A dot therefore pays for itself if and only if its marginal credit exceeds α · DTI — 0.0520 at DTI 0.26, 0.0556 at 0.2778. Equivalently: a dot pays only if it lands within roughly 284 m of a hidden truth pixel.

Read that way, the family’s trajectory 0.1922 → 0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 is not a sequence of discoveries. Emitted mass fell 121,131 → 60,069 → 44,090 → 40,199 → 37,654 — a 69 % cut — while earned credit fell far less. The 0.2778 arm’s specific move was to delete every dot within 2 px of the given catalogue, i.e. every dot sitting on a masked pixel. Masked mass cannot earn credit and still costs α per unit if the mask is applied only to the penalty term. Removing it is free precision.

The cross-check that this is really what happened: the organiser’s masking was confirmed by a natural experiment in the recovered bytes. Hedge-v2 and ens12 are byte-identical off the catalogue and differ by 54,533 dots placed exactly on catalogue pixels — and both returned 0.1563. Masked pixels are dropped before both sums.

Remaining work

  1. The 1 m DEM was never used. The competition supplies links to 716 USGS 3DEP 1 m tiles and this project could only reach the owner’s pre-derived 100 m LiDAR scarp rasters. Scarps are a metre-scale phenomenon; resampling them to 100 m destroys the discriminating signal. This is the single largest untested lever and the most likely explanation for why the top of the board is at 0.3262 while 44 sibling repositories converged on 0.26–0.28.
  2. Three submission slots would identify T, F and K exactly. DTI(λp) = λT/(λα(T+F)+βK) makes 1/DTI linear in 1/λ, so an anchor, a λ = 0.5 scaling and a null-addition of known mass solve for all three in closed form. Designed, never spent. Conditioning is 56×–1380× better than reading four decimal places.
  3. Thread 11527’s staff answer was never retrieved — which data the NLR/USGS experts actually used to identify the new faults. That single post would settle the field question directly. The page renders a collapsed post list; retry via the last-post URL or the ?print=true endpoint.
  4. H47-4 (deep-source magnetic continuity) is specified and its data is already restored (TMI_up150 in geodawn_extensions_u8.tif) but unbuilt.
  5. Whether scoring is restricted to a public chunk footprint is still unresolved. If the whole raster is scored but only public-chunk truth exists, mass in private chunks is pure FP. No prior repository answers this either.

Limitations you should weigh before trusting anything here

  • Nothing was scored. No DrivenData credentials exist in this environment. Every DTI in the F1–F5 columns is a model output, labelled UNSCORED.
  • Thirteen scalars are the entire ground truth. Six of the thirteen are nested thinnings of one field, so the effective sample size is well below thirteen.
  • The 0.2778 attribution is contested. A sibling repository records attribution_conflict_site_says_unscored_official_board_has_unlinked_0.2778_row. That one number decides the flank verdict, which is why the break-even (0.2200) is reported rather than a conclusion.
  • The local frames disagree with each other. SGMC ranks the lattice best (0.2499) although it scored 0.0904 live; the blocked catalogue holdout ranks the flank-pruned arm worst (0.0046) although it reportedly scored highest. No local instrument can settle this.
  • Optimizer’s curse is large and was measured at ≈0.30 DTI. Any in-fold number on this site is decorative.
