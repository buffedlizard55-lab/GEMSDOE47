Hypothesis H50 · round 5

The scarps the catalogue
does not contain.

Every fault population available to this repository is either masked out of the official evaluation or biased toward exposed mountain bedrock. The one population that is both real and positively rank-correlated with the reported leaderboard scores is the set of off-catalogue local maxima of the owner-derived 1 m lidar scarp stack (USGS 3DEP 1 m tiles — corrected 2026-10-07, IR-2026-10-07-B). H50 selects its field against that population, and it beats every control on the spatially blocked holdout.

Reading. The fault-catalogue proxies are negatively correlated, exactly as they must be: their pixels are masked out of evaluation, so mass placed on them is pure waste. The union of the six lidar peak sets is also negative — pooling dilutes the signal. Twenty of the thirty-one lidar-peak parameterisations are positive (sign test across the family), and lappos_max at threshold 200 / min-distance 3 is the strongest single instrument. With thirteen pairs a Spearman ρ of 0.55 is only about p = 0.05, so this is suggestive evidence, not a certification. It is nevertheless the only available instrument whose sign is positive, and it is used here as a proxy, never as a private-label guarantee.

Why the family best (owner-reported 0.2778) looks the way it does. The same ranking explains it without appealing to an authenticated score. That raster has 37,654 dots, none of them within 300 m of the catalogue, and its DTI against the off-catalogue lidar scarp peaks is 0.0494 — higher than spaced random (0.0170) and higher than H47-C1 (0.0483), but only a quarter of what the H50 field reaches (0.1659). So the family best already had the two structural ingredients that matter: sparse 280 m emission and zero mass on the masked catalogue. What it lacked was a field that ranks the off-catalogue scarps. That is the entire content of H50.

The contrarian result. Every multiplicative corroboration with a magnetic, gravity, geodetic, curvature or ruggedness term degrades the field against the off-catalogue scarps. The family's multi-physics AND-gate discards exactly the steep scarps the 1 m lidar finds. What survives is the plain physics: a fault scarp is a locally steep step on an otherwise gentle surface, and the detrended-elevation slope above its own 2.5 km regional level expresses precisely that.

Hypotheses tested and rejected here, recorded so they are not retried: the step-contrast ratio (scarp step divided by regional steepness) reached only 0.10–0.13; strike-aligned anisotropic emission reached 0.265–0.279 against 0.2917 for isotropic; soft continuous values reached 0.262; a 300 m catalogue flank buffer cost 0.002; pre-smoothing the slope before ranking cost up to 0.07.

Step 3 · the operating point

Split conformal, not eyeballing.

Sixty-one contiguous blocks of the competition grid with a 3 px guard, so no pixel and no 300 m kernel support is shared between two blocks. One seeded 50/50 split into a selection half (20 blocks) and a calibration half (21 blocks). The spacing is chosen on the selection half only; the calibration half, which the choice never saw, then certifies it. The bound is the max-residual one-sided split-conformal band of Lei, G'Sell, Rinaldo, Tibshirani and Wasserman (JASA 2018, Algorithm 2 / Theorem 2.2), simultaneous over the whole spacing sweep (evidence/h50/screen.json).

The 90 % bound uses residual rank 20 of 22, so the finite-sample coverage is at least 90.91 % if the block scores are exchangeable — an assumption this repository does not verify and does not claim. At 80 % the same construction gives 0.1534 (rank 18/22, coverage ≥ 81.82 %) and at 75 % it gives 0.1540.

The budget of 37,654 dots is not a leaderboard copy: evidence/h50/budget-profile.json shows the marginal dot's realised kernel weight is still four times the metric's own credit bar (0.2343 against 0.0584) at that mass, and the first 37,654 dots capture 82 % of the achievable DTI gain on the instrument.

WHAT THIS IS NOT

Stated plainly.

  • Instrument L is derived from a lidar product that shares the slope quantity with the field's input band. It is an optimistic instrument for this field; the SGMC instrument is reported alongside it as an independent check and agrees in sign.
  • Spatial separation does not establish geological exchangeability. The conformal floor is a conditional guarantee, not a private-label or leaderboard guarantee.
  • No organiser receipt links any participant score to a TIFF. The 0.2778 figure is owner-reported and the owner's own page marks that submission unscored.
  • The restored data are hash-pinned owner mirrors, not organizer-authenticated bytes.
  • This is a new hypothesis, not a copy of any prior GEMSDOE submission: the uniqueness audit compared the published raster against 31 prior rasters and found zero exact matches with a maximum mask Jaccard of 0.0456.

Reproduce

Five scripts, in order.

  1. scripts/h50_instrument_ranking.py — the instrument ranking and permutation reference.
  2. docs/research/h50-hypotheses-20261007.md — the six ranked candidate hypotheses, written before the field was chosen.
  3. scripts/h50_field_scan.py, scripts/h50_field_refine.py — the field scan and refinement.
  4. scripts/run_h50_screen.py — the blocked holdout and split-conformal selection.
  5. scripts/h50_budget_profile.py — the credit-bar budget analysis.
  6. Historical implementations are preserved in scripts/build_submission_h50.py and scripts/update_site_h50.py, but those entry points are disabled to prevent rebuilding old all-finite output or restoring stale upload instructions.

The detector itself lives in src/gems47/h50.py and is covered by tests/test_h50.py. Generative-AI assistance (Arena.ai coding agent) is disclosed in the repository README.
