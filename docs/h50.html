Hypothesis H50 · round 5

The scarps the catalogue
does not contain.

H50 tests the hypothesis that a fault scarp is a locally steep step on an otherwise gentler surface. In a small repository-local comparison, off-catalogue local maxima from an owner-mirrored 1 m lidar scarp stack were the strongest positively associated proxy population among the tested instruments. That association is exploratory; it does not authenticate any participant score or prove leaderboard relevance. H50's slope-anomaly field beat the stated controls on its documented spatially blocked public-proxy holdout.

Context for the owner-reported 0.2778 figure. A locally mirrored reference file labelled as a d2.8 family-best was evaluated against the H50 public-proxy instrument: it has 37,654 dots, none within 300 m of the catalogue, and pooled DTI 0.0494 — versus 0.0170 for spaced random and 0.0483 for H47-C1. H50 reaches 0.1659 on that same proxy. This is a local file-to-proxy comparison only: the H33/0.2778 participant-score-to-file mapping is unverified, and the owner's page marks the reported submission unscored. It is not evidence of that file's leaderboard performance.

The contrarian result on this proxy. In the tested field family, every multiplicative corroboration with magnetic, gravity, geodetic, curvature or ruggedness terms degraded DTI against these off-catalogue lidar-peak sets. This does not establish that such channels are generally uninformative. The best local proxy result is consistent with a simple hypothesis: a fault scarp can be a locally steep step on a gentler regional surface, and detrended-elevation slope above its own 2.5 km regional level can rank such steps.

Hypotheses tested and rejected here, recorded so they are not retried: the step-contrast ratio (scarp step divided by regional steepness) reached only 0.10–0.13; strike-aligned anisotropic emission reached 0.265–0.279 against 0.2917 for isotropic; soft continuous values reached 0.262; a 300 m catalogue flank buffer cost 0.002; pre-smoothing the slope before ranking cost up to 0.07.

Step 3 · the operating point

Split-conformal diagnostic, not eyeballing.

Sixty-one contiguous blocks of the competition grid with a 3 px guard, so no pixel and no 300 m kernel support is shared between two blocks. One seeded 50/50 split into a selection half (20 blocks) and a calibration half (21 blocks). The spacing is chosen on the selection half only; the calibration half, which the choice never saw, then supplies a conditional lower-bound calculation. The statistic is a max-residual one-sided split-conformal band of Lei, G'Sell, Rinaldo, Tibshirani and Wasserman (JASA 2018, Algorithm 2 / Theorem 2.2), simultaneous over the whole spacing sweep (evidence/h50/screen.json).

Chosen spacing: 2.8 px (280 m) · nominal confidence: 90 % · conditional lower floor: 0.0957 DTI (rank 20/22). The finite-sample coverage calculation is at least 90.91 % if the block scores are exchangeable. Geological/block-score exchangeability is not verified, so this is an assumption-conditional public-proxy diagnostic, not a guaranteed nonzero floor for the competition target. At 80 % the same calculation gives 0.1534 (rank 18/22, coverage ≥ 81.82 %) and at 75 % it gives 0.1540.

The budget of 37,654 dots is not a leaderboard copy: evidence/h50/budget-profile.json shows the marginal dot's realised kernel weight is still four times the metric's own credit bar (0.2343 against 0.0584) at that mass, and the first 37,654 dots capture 82 % of the achievable DTI gain on the instrument.

WHAT THIS IS NOT

Stated plainly.

  • Instrument L is derived from a lidar product that shares the slope quantity with the field's input band. It is an optimistic instrument for this field; the SGMC instrument is reported alongside it as an independent check and agrees in sign.
  • Spatial separation does not establish geological exchangeability. The conformal floor is a conditional guarantee, not a private-label or leaderboard guarantee.
  • No organiser receipt links any participant score to a TIFF. The 0.2778 figure is owner-reported and the owner's own page marks that submission unscored.
  • The restored data are hash-pinned owner mirrors, not organizer-authenticated bytes.
  • This is a new hypothesis, not a copy of any prior GEMSDOE submission: the uniqueness audit compared the published raster against 31 prior rasters and found zero exact matches with a maximum mask Jaccard of 0.0456.
