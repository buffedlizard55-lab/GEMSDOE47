Generated before implementation, as the brief requires. Each names its layers, the physical signature it targets, why it should catch a fault missing from USGS/INGENIOUS rather than one already in it, and how it differs from anything already implemented across the 44 sibling repositories.

The constraint that shaped all five. DrivenData staff, community thread 11536 (chrisk-dd, 23 Sep): “‘new fault’ means ‘any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS’ and can include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system.” Combined with thread 11516 (“Pixels corresponding to known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are masked / excluded from evaluation”), this means known-catalogue pixels are excluded from evaluation. Predictions there cannot earn target credit; no benefit is inferred from deleting them. Every hypothesis below is aimed at off-catalogue geometry by construction.

H47-1 · GSA — geodetic strain × hydrothermal alteration × thermal discharge

Layers. geod_shearrate (band 7) and geod_dilaterate (band 8) from training_features.tif; rad_ThK (band 1 of geodawn_extensions_u8.tif, the GeoDAWN thorium/potassium ratio); and proximity to the 27,092 GDR spring-chemistry and water-well points in gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv.

Physical signature targeted. Not an edge and not a curvature transform — a state signature. Four independent indicators of the same process: (1) crustal strain rate localises on structurally weak, actively slipping zones; (2) hydrothermal fluid flow destroys K-feldspar and Th-bearing accessories and precipitates silica and clay, so altered fault rock reads low Th/K — the fitted coefficient is −5.74, i.e. the truth prefers low Th/K, which is the alteration direction and not a generic “high radiometric contrast” direction; (3) dilatation rate marks volume change at releasing bends and stepovers; (4) warm and hot spring discharge is the surface expression of a permeable fault conduit.

Why it catches a fault MISSING from the catalogue. The given labels are the USGS Quaternary fault compilation plus INGENIOUS — both are surface-rupture compilations, mapped from geomorphology. A fault that is actively straining and hydrothermally altered but has no geomorphic expression (buried under basin fill, or blind beneath a clay cap) is invisible to topographic ridge detection and therefore absent from those compilations, yet it is precisely the fault a geothermal resource assessment wants. Strain rate, alteration chemistry and fluid discharge all see it; a DEM does not.

How it differs from prior work. The sibling collection used radiometrics and thermal points only as corroboration gates on a topographic ridge field — a vote that could only remove dots. This is the first time they are the primary belief field, and the first time the sign of the radiometric term is fitted rather than assumed. LATI fits low Th/K and low tmi_vg, which is the opposite of the “high geophysical contrast” prior every sibling repository used.

Result. The conditional in-fold LOO fit improved +86.4 % (0.014401 → 0.001960) over four forward-selection steps, K = 15,638. The cross-fitted run returned paired out-of-fold deltas −0.0611 and −0.0450, but its observation pool includes the unverified H33-to-0.2778 association, so these values need an H33-excluded sensitivity rerun before they can be interpreted without that assumption. The cross-fit is observation-level, not a spatial holdout. Against the d2.8 reference H47-GSA underperforms on four local frames; its F2 win is on its own in-fold fitted objective and is not independent evidence. It is NOT PROMOTED; no submission is authorized.

H47-2 · SAF — strike-aligned catalogue-flank continuation

Layers. The catalogue mask itself. A structure tensor (σ = 3 px) gives the local strike; distance_transform_edt(return_indices=True) gives, for every pixel, the offset vector to the nearest catalogue pixel; their dot product decomposes the 0–300 m halo into along-strike continuation, across-strike splay, trace-tip extension (weighted by exp(−d_tip/6) over 47,431 detected endpoints) and bend splay (weighted by 1 − coherence). Implementation: src/gems47/hypotheses.py.

Physical signature targeted. Displacement transfer. Normal faults in the Basin and Range grow by linkage of en-echelon segments, so unmapped geometry concentrates at propagation tips (displacement tapering into a monocline or relay ramp) and at releasing-bend stepovers. Both are directional, not buffer-shaped.

Why it catches a fault MISSING from the catalogue. It is the most literal reading of the organiser’s own words — “newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system”. A trace that stops on the map but continues in the ground produces new-fault pixels immediately along-strike of the mapped endpoint, and those pixels are by definition not in USGS/INGENIOUS.

How it differs from prior work. Directly opposite. The H33-2-B2 artifact deletes every dot within 2 px of the catalogue; its association with the participant-level 0.2778 row is unverified. Sibling arms h32-1-prethin-tip-euler (owner-reported 0.2649) and h33d-analog-tip-stepover-r30 (owner-reported 0.2632) grew tips but reportedly scored below their own baselines. This hypothesis says those arms grew tips isotropically instead of along-strike; all score comparisons remain participant/owner-reported unless artifact receipts exist.

Result — unresolved; the H33 score/file association is not authenticated. On twelve owner-reported rows, the exploratory halo fit showed a +55.7 % LOO improvement (β = +6.18); its fitted field placed 67.1 % of modeled K within 150 m of a mapped fault. Adding H33-2-B2 at an assumed DTI of 0.2778 changes the fit to −64.0 % LOO improvement (β = +1.42), but the public 0.2778 participant row is not linked to this TIFF and the owner page labels H33 unscored. The sensitivity sweep uses a coarse grid: LOO improvement is positive at assumed 0.2200 and negative by 0.2400. That brackets a sign change under this model; 0.2200 is not an exact break-even, and no flank verdict follows without a verified mapping and independent validation. The 12-row estimates, permutation checks, and strike-decomposition comparison remain exploratory fits, not evidence of private-target performance. Not shipped.

H47-3 · SGMC transfer — catalogue-difference emission

Layers. derived_sgmc_faults_100m_u8.tif: 83,593 px of USGS State Geologic Map Compilation fault traces rasterised to the 100 m grid, of which 62,122 px lie more than 300 m from the competition’s given catalogue.

Physical signature targeted. None — this is a set difference, not a detector. A fault mapped by a state geological survey and absent from the Quaternary compilation is, by the organiser’s definition, a candidate new fault.

Why it catches a fault MISSING from the catalogue. It does not infer missing faults, it imports them from an official compilation the competition did not supply as labels. Highest possible confidence per pixel: these are mapped, real, published fault traces.

How it differs from prior work. A sibling repository specified this as H60-2 and built sgmc-off-catalogue-44k, but the file was never scored, so the hypothesis was never tested. This project compares it only within an exploratory fit to owner-reported score/raster pairs; those associations are not organizer-authenticated leaderboard labels or private-target truth.

Result — not supported in this exploratory fit; no general falsification claim. Adding SGMC proximity to the owner-reported d2.8-reference + flank model made LOO worse (0.008124 → 0.009279), and the conditional fitted q allocates only 0.5 % of its modeled K to SGMC off-catalogue pixels; this is not a measured hidden-truth proportion. The frame built from it is also self-defeating as a selector: a raster consisting of the SGMC pixels scores 1.0000 on it by tautology, and the near-uniform r13-lattice scores 0.2499 — better than the owner-reported 0.2600 d2.8 reference’s 0.0942 — because the frame mostly measures coverage, not skill. This is a model-specific screening result, not evidence the source can never help. Not shipped.

H47-4 · DMC — deep-source magnetic continuity ALREADY TESTED — FAILED

Layers. TMI_up150 (band 4 of geodawn_extensions_u8.tif, total magnetic intensity upward-continued 150 m) against the supplied 100 m tmi_hg, tmi_vg, rtp and tc bands.

Physical signature targeted. Depth discrimination by continuation contrast. Upward continuation suppresses shallow sources; a lineament that persists at 150 m of continuation is basement-scale, while one that vanishes is near-surface. Faults through alluvium-filled basins are buried: no scarp, no topographic ridge, but a deep magnetic-offset signature that survives continuation. The discriminator is the ratio of continued to uncontinued edge response, not either alone.

Why it catches a fault MISSING from the catalogue. The Quaternary compilation is geomorphology-driven, so a fault with zero surface expression under basin fill is systematically absent from it. An exploratory, H33-conditional LATI fit to owner-reported score/raster pairs associates high tmi_hg (β = +8.55) with low tmi_vg (β = −9.63). That association is qualitatively compatible with the proposed contrast but does not physically validate buried faults or private-target performance. The observation-level cross-fit is conditional on H33 and H47-GSA is not promoted.

How it differs from prior work. Sibling arms used tmi, rtp, tc and tilt-angle zero crossings as edge detectors at a single scale. None used upward continuation as a depth filter, and none combined it with the vertical-gradient band as a contrast pair. H61-3 (tilt-angle zero crossings) is the nearest relative and is a single-scale edge detector.

This hypothesis is not new, and it has already been tested and rejected. While merging the two GEMSDOE47 sessions it emerged that the earlier session built exactly this idea as H47-B — cross-scale persistence of TMI_up150 magnetic edges — and ran it through a preregistered gate with a locked test block and nontrivial controls. The physical rationale remains a hypothesis, not a validated finding. The reported LATI coefficients (tmi_hg β = +8.55; tmi_vg β = −9.63) come from a conditional 13-row fit that assumes the unverified H33 score/file link. A plausible physical story does not overcome H47-B's locked-test loss to the fixed-seed random control. Recorded here so a future session does not treat the idea as untested or promoted.

H47-5 · λ-probe — diagnostic scale-identity concept NOT AUTHORIZED

Layers. None. This is a measurement design, not a detector.

Physical signature targeted. The metric’s own exact scale identity. Both T and F are homogeneous of degree 1 in the prediction, so DTI(λp) = λT / (λα(T+F) + βK) and 1/DTI(λ) is linear in 1/λ. Under the scale assumptions, three score observations could solve for T, F and K in closed form: S1 ANCHOR (λ = 1, a reference raster with an owner-reported 0.2600 score), S2 SCALE (λ = 0.5), and S3 NULL-ADD (the anchor plus 2,000 dots placed where truth is assumed unlikely). The S1 score/file association is not authenticated. The three observations describe the proposed measurement design, not a count of portal uploads or submission slots. The exact current per-user quota and slot accounting are not stated on the accessible official pages checked 2026-10-06; the number of new uploads and their slot cost are therefore unknown. Do not infer a fixed cost or a free route.

Why it matters. The identity is one possible way to estimate T, F and K if the scoring response follows the stated scale law and the reference raster is known. The reported conditioning comparison is algebraic; it does not establish that the probe is useful, non-scarce, or rank-neutral in the live competition.

How it differs from prior work. A sibling repository designed a similar probe set and built three files. This project independently re-derived the identity and tested it numerically to 1e-15 in tests/test_metric.py. No probe was submitted here. The number of new submissions cannot be fixed without an authenticated reusable S1 score; the portal's current quota/accounting must be checked before any experiment. The project gate does not authorize this diagnostic.

Status. Theoretical design only, not run and not authorized. Do not claim a fixed submission-slot cost or that the probe is free. Revisit only after portal/organizer confirmation and separate preregistration; this diagnostic is not a candidate promotion path.

What was screened and rejected

72 layers were screened on twelve owner-reported pairs (59 geological + 13 controls); the 65-layer, 13-row screen adds H33 only under the unverified assumed-0.2778 mapping. These are exploratory low-ranking or negative results, not general falsifications:

  • LiDAR scarp morphology (12 bands derived from 706 USGS 3DEP 1 m tiles): lidar_upface_max ranked 21st of 72 on 12 observations and did not enter the 13-observation forward selection. lidar_coh100, lidar_strike and lidar_valid were among the worst layers. Consistent with the resampling argument: metre-scale scarp morphology averaged to 100 m loses the discriminating signal.
  • Thermal-spring temperature class: thermal_hot_prox was the worst layer on 12 observations (−192 β, SSR 0.2299); thermal_warm_prox (all 27,092 points, not just the hot ones) entered the 12-observation selection at step 3. Broad discharge density matters; temperature class does not.
  • Volcanic vent proximity: 21 vents in the footprint — too few to carry signal.
  • Earthquake density (ieq_n100a15, deq_n100a15): +47.1 % and +26.6 % on 13 observations, but neither entered the forward selection ahead of strain rate.
  • Every gradient of a supplied band (cond_surf_grad, depth_to_base_surf_grad, iso_grav_anom_grad): among the worst on 13 observations (−89.7 %, −72.7 %, −64.4 %). Edge detection on these fields is exhausted.
