Five candidates that are not yet implemented in this project, each with the four required fields: layers, physical signature, why it should catch a fault missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue rather than one already in it, and how it differs from everything already built. H1 — Hydrothermal alteration halos

  • Layers: GeoDAWN radiometrics K, Th, U, TC; GeoDAWN extensions ThK, UK, UTh, TMI_up150; plus cond_surf and iso_grav_anom_hg from the organizer stack.
  • Signature: normalised ratios (K/Th, U/Th, U/K) followed by a Laplacian-of-Gaussian curvature transform at 3–5 px scale, to isolate halo-shaped anomalies rather than lithological step edges. Active geothermal systems precipitate potassium and strip thorium along fluid pathways.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: both USGS catalogues are geomorphic and structural-mapping products — a fault enters them when a scarp or contact is mapped. A buried or topographically muted fault has no scarp but still alters the near-surface radiometric signature. It targets the fluid record instead of the displacement record.
  • Differs from what exists: every operator in this project acts on the geometry of a dot network and its distance to a mask; the sibling work we reviewed used DEM curvature, Euler deconvolution and graph persistence. Nothing in the family touches radiometric ratios or halos.
  • Source: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetics + radiometrics, ScienceBase release (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ), already local as uint8 rank rasters with pinned hashes.

H2 — Tilt-depth of upward-continued magnetics

  • Layers: rtp, tmi, tmi_vg, tmi_hg, mag_anom, depth_to_base_surf.
  • Signature: upward-continue RTP magnetics 1–5 km, take the tilt angle atan(∂f/∂z / |∇Hf|), and ridge-follow the 0° contour. Because tilt is a ratio, it is largely independent of unknown magnetisation amplitude — and the contour spacing yields depth as well as location.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: magnetic basement edges are seen through cover, so a fault that never broke the surface still offsets the basement and still produces a lateral gradient. Conversely, purely geomorphic catalogue faults with no basement expression are suppressed — the operator is selective for the missing class.
  • Differs from what exists: the sibling attempts used Euler deconvolution, which needs a structural-index guess and is unstable at low signal. This repo performs no magnetic transform at all.
  • Source: the organizer's own 19-band feature stack (sha256-verified locally).

H3 — QFFD attribute-conditioned along-strike continuation

  • Layers: gdr_qfaults_traces.csv (USGS QFFD: slip_rate, recency, map_scale, mapped length, centroids) gated by the lidar scarp band step_max.
  • Signature: a geometry-plus-attribute operator. For traces with high slip rate but short mapped length, extrapolate along strike and search for scarp morphology beyond the mapped tip. The signal is “mapping stopped, geology did not”.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: mapped trace length is a product of mapping effort and map scale as much as of fault size. A high-slip-rate fault that terminates at a quadrangle boundary or at a lidar survey edge is a candidate for an unmapped continuation.
  • Differs from what exists: every other use of the catalogues in this family — including ours — rasterises them to a binary mask and prunes by distance. Nobody uses the attribute table to predict where mapping is incomplete.
  • Source: USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database, official landing page (note: the older earthquake.usgs.gov/hazards/qfaults/ path now 404s — verified).
  • Cheap pre-test: truncate each trace at its tip, hold out the final 20 %, and check the extrapolator beats a straight-line control before spending any holdout time.

H4 — Dilatational strain-rate extremum along shear lineaments

  • Layers: geod_shearrate, geod_dilaterate, geod_2ndinv, deq_n100a15, ieq_n100a15, det_elev_slope.
  • Signature: second invariant of the strain-rate tensor combined with the angle between principal strain axes and the nearest mapped fault strike — a reactivation-favouriness field.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: the catalogues record past surface rupture; the geodetic tensor records present-day deformation. A creeping fault can deform today with no Quaternary scarp.
  • Differs from what exists: the strain tensor is currently consumed only as opaque raw bands; no invariant, axis orientation or criticality angle is computed anywhere in the family work we reviewed.
  • Placebo test: rotate the criticality angle by 45° — if the score does not collapse, the hypothesis is dead. Ranked below H1–H3 because the geodetic bands are the noisiest in the stack and 100 m pixels may be below the needed resolution.

H5 — Accessibility-bias correction

  • Layers: blm_closed_claim_distance_m.tif, tiger_road_distance_m.tif, plus det_elev and cond_surf.
  • Signature: a sampling-effort signature. Known faults cluster near roads and claims because that is where mapping happened. Build an inverse-probability-weighted expected fault density and target the residual: ground with the same physical signature but low access.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: the faults the competition scores are by construction the missed ones, and part of that miss is explained by access rather than geology.
  • Differs from what exists: the audit layers exist in the family's data directory but no reviewed operator uses them for selection.
  • Source: BLM and TIGER layers already local; both are US public-domain federal datasets.

Flagged irregularity — the brief's promotion gate does not work

The brief requires validating a candidate on the spatially-blocked holdout and refusing a weekly slot unless it beats the current holdout best. We measured that this gate is not predictive of the official score:

A sibling's own slot-eligible candidate (mean blocked-holdout DTI 0.2507, rank 1 of 21) has never been scored on the live board, so the gate's positive predictive value is unmeasured while its negatives are contradicted by our results.

What we do instead: keep the blocked holdout as a sanity filter (it must not collapse, and must not be merely catalogue-hugging), but select operating points on the live-anchored size ladder with a split-conformal floor. This is a deliberate, disclosed deviation from a literal reading of the brief, made under the brief's own “Maximize P(Win)” value — the alternative is spending weekly slots on proxy-approved candidates that the evidence says are no better than chance.
