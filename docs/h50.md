## Why 0.2778, and can we beat 0.3774?

The supplied score/file association is owner-reported, not organizer-authenticated. The
GEMSDOE32 page inspected this session explicitly labels H33-2-B2 unscored. It describes flank
pruning, not proof that scatter-greedy earned the score. The draft incorrectly conflated those
methods and treated modeled Monte Carlo gains as leaderboard evidence; those claims are withdrawn.

The metric identity is `D=T/[.2(T+F)+.8K]`. Removing redundant prediction mass can improve DTI
if the saved false-positive cost outweighs the lost weighted truth coverage. At fixed truth,
the exact improvement criterion is `deltaT*(1-.2D) - .2D*deltaF > 0`. Pixels excluded from scoring
cannot cause a gain merely by being deleted. Nearby evaluated pixels require paired measurement.
This explains a plausible mechanism for sparse-dot improvements, not the cause of the reported
0.2778. There is no demonstrated universal score ceiling.

At fixed F and K, moving .2778 to .3774 needs about **39% more weighted true credit**:
`[.3774/(1-.2*.3774)]/[.2778/(1-.2*.2778)]`. This is conditional algebra, not a forecast.
A better independent fault detector could beat .3774; this run does not demonstrate one.
See [corrected attribution analysis](why-02778.html) and the
[official metric](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/).
