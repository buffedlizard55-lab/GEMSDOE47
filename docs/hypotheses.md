## H47-B preregistered physical hypothesis and test

**Layers.** The obtainable screen uses the USGS GeoDAWN `TMI_up150` channel in the four-band `geodawn_extensions_u8.tif` mirror. Its band order and rank-encoding are stated in the accompanying `geodawn_extensions.json`; the source is an upward-continued total-magnetic-intensity product. The competition's raw `rtp`, `tmi`, `tmi_vg`, `tmi_hg`, and source-depth features are not present in this checkout.

**Target signature.** A contact-like magnetic edge that persists in location and axial orientation after Gaussian smoothing at 200, 400, and 800 m scales (2, 4, and 8 pixels on the 100 m grid). Persistence across scale is intended to reject isolated, scale-specific texture; it is a **screening transform**, not a physical-depth solution. A real tilt-depth implementation requires unquantized magnetic data and vertical derivatives.

**Why it could find a fault absent from the catalogues.** A buried fault may offset or juxtapose magnetic basement beneath basin fill without producing a mapped surface scarp. The competition's own problem statement says its target faults are expert-identified faults absent from the current public USGS database. This mechanism is plausible, not guaranteed: magnetic contacts also arise from lithologic boundaries, remanent magnetization, and processing artifacts.

**Difference from inspected methods.** The current code has a single-surface acquisition-invariance gradient screen and an H1 radiometric-ratio/LoG halo screen (the latter failed its first mechanism test). The inspected sibling artifacts include Euler/point-source and ridge/scarp methods. H47-B instead ranks locations by *cross-scale persistence and axial agreement* in a magnetic edge field; it does not use catalogue-distance pruning to draw the prediction. This novelty review is bounded to the sources inspected, not every competitor submission.

**Official science basis.** The USGS GeoDAWN release describes magnetic grids and identifies the data as supporting subsurface structure/geology. USGS's Southern Gabbs Valley study reports a blind geothermal system, without surface hot springs/fumaroles/paleo-geothermal deposits, whose favorable setting was recognized using gravity, magnetic and MT evidence. Salem et al.'s peer-reviewed [tilt-depth paper](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.2821934) describes a normalized derivative edge/depth method, but this screen does **not** reproduce that method because raw vertical derivatives are unavailable.

**Frozen screen protocol.** `docs/preregistered-h2.md` records the feature hash, fixed scales, fixed emission budget, spacing sweep, spatial block split, controls, conformal rank rule, and promotion decision rule before scoring. The candidate is generated without labels; the public label raster is used only for the blocked DTI evaluation. A pass can screen the method on public catalogue faults but cannot establish performance on private, newly mapped faults or authorize a competition upload.

## Evidence interpretation and submission gate

- A spatially blocked score against the public catalogue is a screening proxy. The catalogue is incomplete and differs from the private target; even a holdout win would not prove new-fault discovery.
- No authenticated, comparable historic spatial-holdout incumbent was available before H47-B. H47-B is not that incumbent: it failed promotion and is not slot-eligible. The historical d-cat TIFF used catalogue distance in construction and is not a valid independent holdout reference. A reproducible current holdout best remains to be established.
- H47-B selected 5 px / 500 m; locked-test pooled DTI was 0.027553 versus 0.025639 for the single-scale baseline and 0.037159 for random. Five blocks had no catalogue truth, including two test and two calibration blocks. Its nominal 6/7 split-conformal calculation had an unverified exchangeability assumption and a clipped lower floor of zero. Full details are in `docs/validation-h47b-20261006.md`.
- The H47-B mask had zero exact positive-mask matches among 334 accessible, exact-grid-comparable artifacts from visible sibling repositories, but this is not a global uniqueness proof and does not make the mask scientifically eligible.
- Split-conformal lower bounds are finite-sample valid only under the stated exchangeability condition for the chosen unit/target. Spatial dependence and regional nonstationarity make that assumption uncertain; do not describe an assumption-conditional public-block calculation as a guaranteed private leaderboard floor.
- Do not spend a submission slot unless a genuinely new candidate beats the established current spatially blocked holdout best and appropriate controls under a preregistered rule, with adequate label coverage and a defensible positive floor if required. If any gate fails, retain the artifact only as research and keep the slot closed.
